# Atlas HUD (frontend/index.html)

Mode: Operate. Audience: the owner, daily, desktop, dim room. Job: chat with a local model and see which Atlas modules exist yet.

## Direction contract

THESIS: Atlas's chat lives inside a holographic HUD whose core is the model itself: the core is a holographic Earth that talks while the model streams (ripples + voice bars per chunk), its rings spin at the measured stream rate, and modules light up only for backend routes that really exist. Refuses the category default of a sidebar + centred transcript chat clone.

OWN-WORLD: Blue-black projection ground with a faint grid; cyan ink (#d8f6ff body, #3ad8ff signal, near-white core), amber for stubs/warnings, red for failure. Saira variable: expanded width, tracked caps for panel heads and readouts; normal width for reading. Panels are 1px cyan frames with corner brackets; glow only on the core, clock and live values.

STORY: The owner sees the link is up, which model answers, and what he has built. He types; the reply streams into the log while the core reacts; he reads it comfortably.

FIRST VIEWPORT: (Revised by the owner: less clutter.) Minimal top bar (Systems drawer button, Atlas wordmark, link chip, clock). Centred: a holographic dot-matrix Earth inside the rotating rings (~41dvh), state readout under it, then the chat box (session log + composer, max 780px) filling the rest. No module nodes on the ring, no suggested prompts. Telemetry and modules live in a left Systems drawer (tabs: Telemetry | Modules).

FORM: Pinned by the owner (JARVIS reference image); position 1 of 1. seed: none (roll skipped: direction pinned by the owner, in his words: "I want it to look like Jarvis from IRON MAN. I gave you an image"). Signature interaction: the Earth talks while Atlas replies (chunk-driven ripples across the land dots and voice bars around the rim), the thinking scan arc sweeps while waiting, and ring speed follows chunks/s.

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance
